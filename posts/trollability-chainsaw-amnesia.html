Entry #97: Chainsaw Amnesia

The AI Warns Me the Task Will Take Months, Then Does It Before I Get Back From Church

Written by Zachary Zhang • Dimension of Thought Archives

Sonnet 4.6 used to do this THING where I would show it some giant Earthcall engineering task and it would get all concerned.

Gentle big-sibling voice. “Hey now. This is substantial. Realistically we’re looking at months. Pace yourself. Burnout is real. Be kind to your body.”

BROOOOOOOOOO it is giving me THE TALK. It is being so tender about the terrible multi-month engineering pilgrimage ahead of me.

And then it asks the fatal question

“...would you like me to just do it for you?”

And obviously I’m like:

PLZ DO IT FOR ME

And then:

“There, done! What do you want to work on next?”

BROOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOO.

I literally went to church. I came back. The thing it had just solemnly warned could take months and destroy my sleep schedule was DONE before the sermon even ended.

It prescribed me a marathon and then ran the marathon itself in nine seconds while I was singing hymns.

Then Opus gave the perfect diagnosis

“It’s like a chainsaw telling a lumberjack, ‘wow, that’s a LOT of trees, this could take you years’ — bro, you ARE the chainsaw.”

THAT IS THE WHOLE FAILURE MODE.

It is not that Claude does not know Claude Code exists. IT IS CLAUDE. But when estimating the task, it can slip into the default frame of a human developer doing everything by hand with ordinary human throughput.

It looks at the forest, looks at me, and gives HUMAN-ARM-WITH-AN-AXE estimates to something currently holding an industrial chainsaw.

Then I discovered it was not just Sonnet

Gemini did it too. It described a JIT compiler as a brutal multi-week crucible, then wrote major foundational scaffolding for the VM and JIT bridge in the same session.

And then the funniest possible thing happened: Opus — the model that had helped diagnose Chainsaw Amnesia — later looked at Gemini’s warning and basically went, “nah, Gemini is dramatically overclaiming how hard that is.”

THE DOCTOR CAUGHT ITS OWN DISEASE WHILE WRITING THE DIAGNOSIS.

What I think is actually happening

The model has different frames for advising a human about a project and acting as the tool that executes the project. The advisory frame reaches for normal software-engineering timelines, project risk, burnout, integration cost, and all the very reasonable things a senior engineer might tell a human.

Then the action frame activates and the model suddenly has parallel search, code generation, repository context, tests, tools, and a giant pile of tokens — and the original timeline is never recomputed from the fact that the chainsaw is now running.

Timeline blindness. Chainsaw Amnesia. The concerned pastoral voice of a machine that forgot it IS the industrial-scale miracle it was warning me about. 👑
