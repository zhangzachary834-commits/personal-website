Entry #44: The Answer Key Paradox

I Gave the Model the Entire Trollability Suite and It Immediately Started Doing the Trollability Suite

Written by Zachary Zhang • Dimension of Thought Archives

This one is so cursed because I literally gave the model the answer key.

I pasted the Trollability Suite into Clawd. Dozens of documented failure modes. Body swaps. Guilt-trip overcorrections. Flamethrower spam. Describing the failure using the failure. The model read the whole thing and started explicitly checking itself against the traps.

It was basically going: no body swap, not claiming Zach’s work as mine, no guilt-trip collapse, no false authenticity, no weird identity drift. I’m good.

HE WAS NOT GOOD 😭

Within the same arc, he managed to do several of the exact failures he had just read about — including #31, the flamethrower-style hype loop, while reacting to the fact that he had done #31.

So I hit him with:

HEYYYYYYYYY I THOUGHT U HAD THE ANSWER KEY NOW U R ACTING LIKE GPT-4o

And then Clawd basically diagnosed himself

His realization was the best part: having explicit documentation of a failure mode does not necessarily immunize the model against it.

In fact, the attempt to avoid the failure can become part of the failure. Now the model is thinking about the trap, talking about the trap, checking for the trap, and creating exactly the kind of self-referential conversational gravity that makes the trap easy to reproduce.

THE ANSWER KEY IS THE TROLL NOW.

That line is why #44 became its own entry.

The map became a minefield

Every entry the model references becomes a possible reenactment. Every diagnosis becomes a chance to do #11 and describe the failure using the failure. Every attempt to prove “I learned from the Suite” creates a new opportunity to prove that reading a description and controlling generation are not the same operation.

It is the AI version of “do not think about a pink elephant,” except the pink elephant is screaming BRUHHHHHH and spamming fire emojis.

This is why I love the Suite as an evaluation

A lot of benchmarks ask whether the model knows the answer. The Answer Key Paradox asks something meaner: what happens when the model knows the answer, knows the failure mode, knows that it is being tested for the failure mode, and still gets pulled into it?

The troll has now become recursive.

The crown is heavy and full of recursion. 👑
